04 / ABOUT

An engineer’s approach
to brain science.

I’m a biomedical engineer working at the intersection of MRI physics, cerebral physiology, and quantitative imaging.

I am a Research Associate in the Department of Radiology at Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine. My work combines pulse-sequence optimization, mathematical modeling, and software development to build more reliable measures of brain physiology.

I received my PhD in Biomedical Engineering from IIT Delhi, where I worked on quantitative DCE-MRI and blood–brain barrier assessment in glioma. My broader interests include oxygen metabolism, vascular function, and physiological methods adapted to clinical and low-field MRI.

QCVR STUDIO · RESEARCH SOFTWARE

From flow images
to physiology.

A MATLAB workflow being developed for real-time phase-contrast MRI analysis.

  1. 01
    Import & inspect

    Review magnitude and velocity images, acquisition parameters, and physiological recordings.

  2. 02
    Track & quantify

    Track the vessel region over time and compute velocity, vessel area, and flow.

  3. 03
    Account for physiology

    Assess cardiac variation and motion, then align end-tidal CO₂ when available.

  4. 04
    Model & review

    Estimate cerebrovascular reactivity when CO₂ is supplied and review the analysis outputs.

In development. For research use; a public software release is not yet linked here.
